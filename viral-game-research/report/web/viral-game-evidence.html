Research brief · October 2026

What to build to reach 1M daily players in 90 days

Evidence from 126 casual games and their clones, SEC filings, Google Trends and a census of 133 Wordle-likes. The goal is a solo-built game with no paid marketing.

  1. There is no stable recipe, and simplicity doesn't separate hits from flops.A model of the textbook viral recipe, trained on 2008–2015 hits, ranks 2016–2025 games worse than a coin flip (AUC 0.28). The recipe gets copied, the copies flop, and the next hits come from a different channel.
  2. Watchability, a channel with spare reach, and retention do hold up.Watchability was the one pre-registered new signal to pass the held-out test, both as I hand-coded it and as an objective YouTube-vs-web search measure. It sparks growth but doesn't sustain it.
  3. 1M daily players in 90 days is reachable. One channel produces it regularly today.It needs a doubling every 8–9 days from a 1,000-player seed. Roblox produced several zero-ad games that did it in 2025. Grow a Garden went from about 1,000 to 21.3M concurrent players in 87 days.
  4. Build on Roblox, keep it, and take a partner's stake rather than selling.44% of Roblox revenue comes through the Apple and Google stores, so it is effectively mobile. Expect about $4.4M a year at 1M DAU, mostly in year one, because Roblox hits keep only ~8% of their peak a year later.
  5. Top three concepts: Hide as a Painting, Pictionary Obby, Telephone Pictionary Chains.Each can be demand-tested in 1–2 weeks for under $100. The pass and kill thresholds come from the 90-day math.

01 · Your hypothesis

"A repeatable recipe, with simplicity at its core" doesn't survive the data

Simplicity is common to the whole category, so it can't pick winners. 64 of 126 games are simple on all three counts: one input, sessions of 5 minutes or less, first fun within 10 seconds. They hit 69% of the time, against 56% for the rest. The 20 simple non-hits are nearly all clones: Absurdle, Dordle, Worldle, Splashy Fish, Swing Copters. Many of the biggest hits aren't simple at all, including Among Us, FarmVille, Pokémon GO, Draw Something and Grow a Garden.

Timing and channels did much of the work.

  • A natural experiment. Facebook cut app notifications in early 2010, and Zynga's average daily players fell from 67M to 48M that year with the same games. Zynga's S-1 says so directly: the changes "limited the level of communication among users… the number of our players on Facebook declined".
  • The same mechanic wins or loses with the channel. OMGPOP's Draw My Thing (web, 2008) went nowhere, while the same idea on iPhone, Draw Something (2012), reached about 14M DAU. Niantic's Ingress stayed niche; the same mechanic plus Pokémon became Pokémon GO.
  • IP alone isn't enough. Harry Potter: Wizards Unite earned about $12M in its first month against Pokémon GO's ~$300M, and it closed in 2022.
  • New channels pay. Games launched on a new or newly opened channel hit 87% of the time, against 43% on saturated ones.
  • Followers often beat originals. 2048 over Threes, FarmVille over Farm Town, Mafia Wars over Mob Wars, Suika over Merge Big Watermelon. Stumble Guys reached 20M DAU on mobile, the platform Fall Guys skipped.
  • Creators repeating their own recipe needed distribution. 7 of 12 follow-ups hit, and all 7 had a publisher's cross-promotion behind them. Swing Copters, launched on Flappy Bird's fame alone, faded.

On influencers

Influencers mostly amplified growth that had already started. Flappy Bird was US #1 on Jan 17, 2014, and PewDiePie's video went up Jan 27. Words with Friends' founders saw growth inflecting before John Mayer's 2009 tweet. The cases where streamers came first are recent and streamer-native: Among Us, Suika Game and Grow a Garden.

02 · Case studies from daily search data

What actually happened to Wordle, Flappy Bird and Among Us

Did Wordle's share grid cause the spread, or follow it?

Both, in sequence.

  • Spread started without a grid. On Nov 1, 2021 there were 90 players, who came through a family group chat and a footnote in an NYT newsletter.
  • A player invented the grid. Someone in New Zealand typed the emoji grid by hand and others copied it.
  • Wardle then shipped the share button. His announcing tweet, which credits her, timestamps to 2021-12-16 14:54 UTC.

Lesson: the grid made existing word of mouth cheaper and more visible. It didn't create it, and the copies never worked (see section 04).

Did it sell for over $1 million? Yes. The NYT announced the price as "in the low seven figures". The NYT's 10-K says only that it acquired Wordle, because the price was immaterial next to the ~$550M paid for The Athletic. What the NYT got shows in its Q1 2022 filing: "Wordle brought an unprecedented tens of millions of new users to The Times… our best quarter ever for net subscriber additions to Games."

Flappy Bird sat unnoticed for months. What changed?

  • May 24, 2013Released. 13 reviews by Oct 31; a bug-fix update in September.
  • Nov 4First tweet. US game charts at #1,368.
  • Dec 5–22US search first registers on Dec 5, then rises about 11x in two weeks. Top 250 free apps on Dec 13; frustrated teen tweets; school holidays.
  • Jan 10–17, 2014US top 10, then #1. Search doubling every 3.1–3.7 days.
  • Jan 27PewDiePie's video. Growth slows to doubling every 7.9 days.
  • Feb 9Nguyen pulls the game.

Among Us: two years unnoticed, then streamers country by country

03 · The scoring model, judged on games it hadn't seen

Every model variant fails the held-out test I set in advance

I coded all 126 games on the same 20 variables and committed the pass bar to git before running any test. To pass, a model needs a held-out AUC confidence interval above 0.5 on the time split and leave-one-cohort-out AUC above 0.6. AUC measures how often a model ranks a real hit above a non-hit: 0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 is perfect.

Where it fails: false positives

Almost all are 2022–25 Wordle-likes, scored 0.64–0.95: Costcodle, LoLdle, Worldle, Travle, Contexto, Semantle, Nerdle, Globle.

Where it fails: false negatives

The streamer and Roblox era, scored 0.01–0.08: Goose Goose Duck, Dead Rails, 99 Nights, Doors, Stumble Guys, Among Us, Fall Guys, Grow a Garden.

The clone paradox. 114 hit/non-hit pairs in the same cohort have identical recipe features, including 40 daily-word pairs and 35 one-tap pairs. Nothing built from mechanics can tell Flappy Bird from Splashy Fish, or Wordle from Lewdle.

04 · Looking for a signal the listicles miss

Watchability holds out of sample. The famous emoji grid works backwards.

  • Streamer pickup is a property of the game, not luck. Most articles treat a streamer finding a game as chance. The data say streamers pick games that are fun to watch, and you can test for that before building.
  • Roblox publishes what its recommender rewards: "qualified play-through rate, 7-day play days per user, and 7-day intentional co-play days". Watchable co-play games produce exactly those signals.
  • The trade-off: watchable hits keep 12% of their peak interest a year later, against 29% for the rest. Design for retention too.
  • The share grid has a reversed sign. Games with an intriguing share artifact hit 28% of the time against 67% without, because hundreds of clones copied it.

05 · Epidemiology and DAU arithmetic

You need a doubling every 9 days, and retention does most of the work

The model is simple. Every active player-day brings in κ new installs, so the k-factor equals κ times the expected active days per install. For share-on-play games, retention multiplies virality. Going from 1,000 to 1M daily players in 90 days takes a doubling every 9.0 days or faster, held for three months.

Retention scenarios (D1 / D7 / D30): median mobile 22/4/0.8%, top-quartile casual 32/12/4%, strong casual hit 45/20/10%, daily ritual 55/40/28% (assumed). The first two come from GameAnalytics benchmarks.

Real hits ran at k ≈ 1.1–1.6 per share generation, with very short generations. Nobody needs a k-factor of 3. What matters is a loop that turns over within a day or two, plus retention. These rates include press and algorithm pushes, so they overstate person-to-person spread.

06 · The short seller's view

Most attempts never register, and most hits fade within a year

Peaks are worth less than they look:

  • Draw Something lost 33% of its daily players in 30 days.
  • Hamster Kombat lost 86% after its token airdrop.
  • Steal a Brainrot fell about 99% from 25.4M concurrent players.
  • HQ Trivia went from 2.38M concurrent to shutdown in two years.
  • Zynga paid $183.1M for OMGPOP and wrote off $95.5M six months later.

Why rituals last, from behavioral science.

  • Habit formation: habits take a median of 66 days to form (Lally et al. 2010), and attach to stable cues like a group chat (Wood & Neal 2007).
  • Scarcity raises value (Worchel et al. 1975).
  • Near-misses sustain play (Clark et al. 2009), which is Flappy Bird's pipe.
  • Goal gradient: effort rises near a goal (Kivetz et al. 2006), which is Grow a Garden's growing plants.
  • Competence, autonomy and relatedness predict continued play (Ryan et al. 2006).

One warning from the data: a daily-sync hook alone didn't make hits last (23% vs 22% a year on).

07 · Revenue and exits

Build on Roblox and keep it

What buyers paid for

These buyers report and price on: DAU, MAU, unique payers, bookings per DAU, revenue concentration (Candy Crush was 78% of King's bookings), the payback period on paid user acquisition (Rovio: 8–10 months), and conversion to subscribers (NYT).

Recommendation

Platform: Roblox, which is effectively mobile.

  • It's the only channel in the data still producing zero-ad 1M+ DAU breakouts several times a year.
  • Its recommender rewards the co-play and retention signals that held up out of sample.
  • It hosts for free and handles payments.

Sell or keep: keep.

  • A kept Roblox hit is worth about 1.07x its first year: about $4.7M over five years from 1M average DAU, versus $7.1M for a ritual-grade game.
  • Selling outright beats keeping only at one year's revenue or more.
  • Audience-only exits pay about $0.5–1 per daily player (Wordle).
  • The realistic deal is a minority stake to an established Roblox studio for live-ops capacity and reach, which is what Grow a Garden did at ~1,000 concurrent players. Sequels extend the run: Roblox's Q2 2026 letter says "Grow a Garden 2" climbed quickly.

Risks: platform dependence, a young audience (COPPA and moderation), and fast decay.

08 · Concepts

20 concepts scored against the model, and three to test first

Each concept is coded on the same variables as the dataset and scored by three model variants, rank-averaged. Daily-grid web puzzles land at the bottom, matching the clone cohort that failed. The top two tie; Hide as a Painting ranks first because it avoids drawing moderation and stroke-physics risk.

Pass and kill thresholds for every test

You need about +70–80% more new players week over week. At small scale that takes one of two combinations:

  • D7 retention of at least 20%, with each active player bringing at least 0.33 new players a day; or
  • D7 of at least 40%, with at least 0.21.

Below that, the game can still make money, but it won't reach 1M DAU in 90 days without ads.

1

Hide as a Painting

Roblox · model score 0.92 · ~6 weeks to build

Prop hunt in a haunted museum. Hiders pick a frame or pedestal and pose as a painting or statue. Guards with flashlights get three minutes and a limited number of accusations. A new gallery layout each day is shared by every server, with a "best hiding spot of the day" board.

Why it scores high

  • High spectator value: reveals make good clips.
  • Failure comedy, like a "painting" caught mid-twitch.
  • Emergent output: absurd avatar portraits.
  • Borrowed rules: hide-and-seek and Statues.
  • Co-play with real friends.

Risk

  • Prop hunts exist on Roblox. The museum and posing need to read in a 10-second clip.

Cheapest demand test · ~10 days · $0–50

  1. Days 1–3: three 15-second greybox clips on TikTok and Shorts. Kill if none beats your median post.
  2. Days 4–10: one map, one round loop, 30–50 players from Discord. Pass at D1 ≥ 35%, sessions ≥ 10 min, ≥ a third in parties.
  3. Optional $50 sponsored-ads cohort, purely to measure retention.
  4. Weeks 3–6: keep going only at +70% organic visits a week, two weeks running.
2

Pictionary Obby

Roblox · model score 0.92 · ~8 weeks to build

The artist draws a secret word. The strokes become the platforms of an obstacle course that teammates run while guessing the word.

Why it scores high

  • High spectator value.
  • Emergent, funny courses.
  • Borrowed rules: Pictionary.
  • Co-play.

Risks

  • Moderating freehand drawings on a kids' platform: stroke limits, filtering, report tools.
  • Turning strokes into fair platforms. Prototype this first.

Cheapest demand test · ~2 weeks · $0–50

  1. Days 1–4: stroke-to-platform prototype plus the same three-clip test.
  2. Days 5–14: private 4–8-player lobbies from Discord. Same retention and party bars as #1, plus ≥ 3 rounds per session.
3

Telephone Pictionary Chains

Web · model score 0.86 · ~4 weeks to build

An asynchronous telephone game for group chats. You draw a prompt in 60 seconds, a friend captions it, the next person draws the caption. After six steps the chain plays back to the group as a 6-second GIF. No accounts, and almost no cost per player.

Why it scores high

  • An invite loop built into play.
  • Borrowed rules: the Telephone family.
  • High spectator value, failure comedy and emergent output.
  • Async removes Gartic Phone's need for everyone to be online at once.

Risk

  • Weak evidence that web breaks out without ads.

Cheapest demand test · ~1 week · ~$10

  1. Build the 6-step chain and GIF playback.
  2. Seed it in 10 of your own group chats and 3 subreddits.
  3. Every link carries the sender's ID, so the k-factor is counted, not estimated. Pass at ≥ 0.33 new players per active player per day with D7 ≥ 20%. Kill below 0.2.

Order of work

  1. Run #3's one-week test first. It gives you a real, measured share rate by day 7.
  2. Run #1's clip test in parallel.
  3. Build whichever Roblox concept passes.
  4. If it breaks out, bring in a live-ops partner for a minority stake and plan the sequel before the decay sets in.

09 · Limits and sources

What could change these conclusions

  • Coding bias. One coder who knew the outcomes. Pre-registration and the objective watch-ratio check reduce this but don't remove it.
  • Small sample. 126 games, so confidence intervals are wide, and the watch-ratio pass is narrow (lower bound 0.51).
  • Outcome tiers. Some rest on press reports and creator claims. Heardle was moved up a tier after a Trends cross-check and its creator's figure of over 1M plays a day.
  • Google Trends' blind spots. It overweights web games people search for daily, floors small values to zero, and may understate play inside the Roblox app.
  • The Roblox random sample can't see the top of the distribution.
  • Still unknown: per-game revenue per player on Roblox, and Wordle's exact price.
  • Out of scope: PC/Steam co-op hits, which are outside your web/mobile brief.
